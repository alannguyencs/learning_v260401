# Predicate

The relationship graph is powerful but heavy: you have to **build and store the whole map**, keep every line up to date as people move teams or hand off documents, and walk it for each request — extra machinery (often a separate graph database) that's easy to get out of sync. And `/librarian` doesn't actually need all that, because most checks only follow **one short link** ("did *you* write this?", "do *you* manage this person?"), not a long chain. So instead of storing the map, `/librarian` replaces each link with a small yes/no question you can ask on the spot and reuse anywhere — like "is this your own row?", "is this person someone you manage?", or "is anyone logged in at all?". Nothing to store or keep in sync — you just ask the question against the live data the moment you need an answer, and string a few together with "or" so that passing *any one* lets you through. Same idea as following lines on the graph, just far lighter. *Example:* to see a salary row, the rule is "is it your own salary?" *or* "are you this person's manager?" — Alan passes for his own pay and for Chloe's (he manages her), but not for a stranger's.
